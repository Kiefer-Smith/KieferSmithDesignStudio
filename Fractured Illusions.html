Kiefer Smith

Junior Game Designer

Fractured Illusions

All Roles • 1 Year • Unreal Engine 4 • Solo Project • PC • First Person Horror

Overview

This project was a solo-developed first-person horror game created over the course of a year. The game focuses on delivering an immersive and atmospheric experience through detailed level design, environmental storytelling, and carefully crafted gameplay mechanics that build tension and suspense. Small puzzles and exploration elements are integrated to enhance player engagement and encourage discovery within the eerie game world.

Responsibilities

  • Level layout
  • Jumpscare placement & pacing balance
  • Navigation clarity & player flow
  • Puzzle and exploration design
  • Internal playtesting iteration

Design Challenge

The main challenge was creating a cohesive and immersive horror experience as a solo developer. Balancing atmospheric tension with engaging gameplay mechanics, while ensuring clear navigation and player flow, required careful design iteration and attention to detail throughout the development process.

Design Approach

I focused on crafting detailed environments that told a story through visual cues and environmental storytelling. Jumpscare placement was carefully balanced to maintain tension without overwhelming the player, while puzzles and exploration elements were designed to encourage engagement and discovery. Regular playtesting allowed me to iterate on level layouts and pacing to enhance the overall horror experience.

Layout Iteration

At the start of development, I created a greybox layout to establish the basic structure of the levels and test player flow. Through playtesting, I identified areas where navigation was unclear or pacing felt off. I iterated on the level design by adjusting sightlines, jumpscare placement, and environmental details to enhance immersion and improve player experience. This iterative process was crucial in refining the game's atmosphere and ensuring that players felt engaged and immersed in the horror experience. For the switches that the player has to press to open doors a red light was added above them to make them more visually distinct and easier to find, which was a common piece of feedback I received during playtesting. As well as this blood footprints were added to the floor to lead the player towards important areas and encourage exploration.

Iteration Process

Regular playtesting sessions allowed me to gather feedback on level layouts, pacing, and player experience. I made adjustments to jumpscare placement, environmental details, and puzzle design based on this feedback to enhance immersion and improve the overall horror experience. Iteration focused on balancing tension with engaging gameplay mechanics while ensuring clear navigation and player flow throughout the game.

Outcome

The final game successfully delivers an immersive and atmospheric horror experience, with detailed level design, engaging gameplay mechanics, and a cohesive narrative told through environmental storytelling. Player feedback highlighted the effective use of tension and pacing, as well as the engaging puzzles and exploration elements that encouraged discovery within the eerie game world. I would like to return and remake this game in the future as I feel that with more time and resources I could really expand on the core mechanics and create a much richer and more engaging horror experience. The original idea was a three floor hospital with the player having to collect notes and diary entries to piece together the story of what happened in the hospital, but due to time constraints I had to cut the game down to a single floor and remove the collectible story elements.
