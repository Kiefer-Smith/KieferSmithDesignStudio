Project Overview

Tails of Courage is my ongoing MSc capstone project developed in Unity. The project was created to expand my skills in game design, gameplay programming, and mobile game development by designing and building a complete game from concept through to implementation. My goal is to create a polished experience that is enjoyable on Android devices while also being designed with the potential to be ported to consoles in the future.


The game is a child-friendly 2D pixel-art platformer that combines the nostalgic feel of classic retro games with modern gameplay mechanics and responsive controls. It is designed to be easy to pick up, accessible to a wide audience, and enjoyable whether players want a short casual session or a more competitive challenge through replaying levels and improving their performance.


While platforming forms the core gameplay, Tails of Courage introduces mechanics inspired by other genres to keep the experience varied. Players explore handcrafted woodland environments, collect acorns and hidden collectibles, solve environmental puzzles, gather ammunition, battle enemies, recover health through collectible items, climb through vertical areas, and face increasingly challenging platforming sections. Some levels also introduce a One Button Gauntlet mode, where the squirrel automatically runs forward and players must rely on precise jump timing to survive fast-paced obstacle courses.

Design Challenge

The main challenge was delivering meaningful level progression within strict time constraints while maintaining readability on small mobile screens. Unfortunately we encountered a bug which broke the shooting and moving animatons and the player wasn't able to shoot. Which we ended up having to remove the boss encounter.

Design Approach

I structured levels around clear visual landmarks and controlled enemy density to manage pacing. Sightlines were carefully designed to reduce player confusion and support intuitive navigation.

Level Layout Iteration

Early layouts suffered from pacing spikes and unclear navigation. By restructuring enemy spacing and improving landmark visibility, player flow became more readable and consistent.

Iteration Process

Through playtesting, I adjusted hazard placement and made collectible rewards more visible to enhance player engagement and flow. Iteration focused on balancing challenge with readability for short play sessions.

Outcome

The final build delivered tighter encounter pacing and improved spatial readability. Structured iteration significantly enhanced player flow and overall engagement during short play sessions.
