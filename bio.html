Hello

I am a beginner indie game developer with a passion for everything about games, from the weight the story can have on you to the beautiful worlds that can be created for you to get lost in, the music the game has that you feel the moment you are in, as well as how the game can challenge you. Best of all, this creates unforgettable memories that you can always look back on. I am drawn to the type of games that make you feel something, whether it's sneakily exploring a jungle as Naked Snake in Metal Gear Solid or trying to survive in a zombie-infested mansion while scared for your life in Resident Evil. Or just relaxing and playing a stress-free game such as PowerWash Simulator!

From playing games for fun to obsessing over how good games look by studying their mechanics and examining the nice textures, I wanted to create my own games. I aimed to create stories and little experiences that anyone could engage with and enjoy. Ever since my introduction to game design in college, I felt that primarily level and game design was my calling. I would spend hours after college teaching myself how everything works and how each area of a level can have its own story due to the assets and textures. In college, we used UDK, and I taught myself how to use Unreal Engine 4, and now I am using Unreal Engine 5.

By the time I was at Dundalk Institute of Technology, I was eager to make a game, and I got to do just that for a project. By using Unreal, I learned about textures , assets, and blueprints to create mechanics that trigger in a game, such as the player taking damage. I find the blueprint feature in Unreal to be easier to understand and explain, rather than being confused with code, which was a weak point in college.

As a beginner with some experience, my current projects are designed to be small and fun. I am using these projects as a way to improve-or level up, if you will. Words can't describe how I felt when my little horror project turned out well; I managed to complete it in time, and the people who tested it before its release reacted very positively to it. When it was released for the FIS expo, it was enjoyable to stand aside and watch people play, observing how they reacted to the jump scares and how they maneuvered around the map. Watching their thought process was both funny and a cool experience.

When I am not working on games, I am watching content on how games evoke certain feelings in players and why specific features were added. Along with this, I would be playing through new games and analyzing why they did what they did in such specific areas. From annoying my friends by studying textures rather than playing the objective.(Sorry!)

I hope to one day be a part of a team rather than being a solo dev and hoping to be part of much bigger games, aiming for that childhood dream to come true!

This site is a home for my projects, experiments, and the messy but magical process of learning game development. Thanks for stopping by-feel free to look around or reach out if you want to talk about games, jam ideas, or unusual mechanics! Many more games are on the horizon; I can guarantee you that! This is just the beginning.
